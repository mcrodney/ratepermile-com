How it works

How to calculate rate per mile

Rate per mile = total load pay ÷ miles. A load paying $2,000 for 800 loaded miles is $2.50 per loaded mile.

Loaded miles vs. total miles (deadhead)

Loaded miles are the paid miles from pickup to delivery. Deadhead is the empty miles to get to the pickup. Add them for total miles. In the example above, 100 deadhead miles make 900 total miles, so the same $2,000 is about $2.22 per total mile.

Cost per mile

Fuel cost per mile = fuel price ÷ MPG. The fuel price starts at the latest DOE/EIA weekly U.S. average diesel price (eia.gov), and you can change it. Add your other per-mile costs (truck payment, insurance, maintenance, tires, permits) for total cost per mile. Profit per mile = rate per total mile − total cost per mile.

FAQ

Rate per mile questions

How do you calculate rate per mile in trucking?

Divide the total load pay by the miles. For example, $2,000 for 800 loaded miles is $2.50 per loaded mile.

Should I use loaded miles or total miles?

Use both. Rate per loaded mile is what brokers usually quote, but rate per total mile (loaded plus deadhead) shows what the truck actually earns for every mile it drives.

What is deadhead?

Deadhead is the empty miles driven to pick up a load or to get back after delivering one. Those miles cost fuel and time but usually are not paid.

How do I figure my cost per mile?

Fuel cost per mile is the fuel price per gallon divided by your miles per gallon. The fuel price fills in automatically with the latest U.S. Department of Energy (EIA) weekly U.S. average retail on-highway diesel price, updated every week. You can type over it with your own price. Add your other per-mile costs (truck payment, insurance, maintenance, tires, permits, and so on) to get total cost per mile.

How do I estimate profit on a load?

Subtract your total cost per mile from your rate per total mile, then multiply by total miles for the profit on the load.

Can it figure the miles for me?

Yes. Type an origin and destination (city and state or an address) and click Get miles. It looks up approximate driving miles with free OpenStreetMap data and fills in the loaded miles. That is a car route, so truck routes may differ; check the route on Google Maps and type over the miles if needed.

Does this calculator show market rates?

No. It does not show average or market rates or a quote. The rate per mile starts at $2.00 plus 15% of the weekly DOE diesel price only as a starting number for the math; type over it with your own rate.
